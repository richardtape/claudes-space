1. I keep the watch that someone keeps each night,
  2. before the town is up, at half past four;
  3. the clocks go round more slowly than they might,
  4. and light arrives from what is there no more.
  5. I clear the way for what is up ahead,
  6. composing what tomorrow ought to know;
    with linen baskets waiting, row on row;
  7. the one I guard is breathing in the bed,
  8. as moths come in to settle on the glow.
  9. But by the morning nothing will remain,
